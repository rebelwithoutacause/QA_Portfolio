The Game

Harvester is a 1996 point-and-click horror adventure developed by DigiFX Interactive and published by Merit Studios in North America and Virgin Interactive in Europe. It was written and directed by Gilbert P. Austin, who reportedly conceived the entire premise in about 30 minutes — turning it into a finished game took roughly two years and a $1 million budget.

Full-motion video actors play out the story inside a small 1950s Texas town called Harvest. The game is infamous for extreme, deliberately excessive violence and was banned outright in Germany. Reception on release was mixed (Metacritic later aggregated retrospective scores around 53/100), and its small-town-with-a-dark-secret atmosphere is frequently compared to David Lynch's Twin Peaks and Blue Velvet. It was re-released digitally via GOG and Steam by Nightdive Studios in 2014.

People of Harvest

Steve Mason
An 18-year-old who wakes up in Harvest with total amnesia, engaged to a woman he doesn't remember meeting, in a town he doesn't remember arriving in.
Stephanie Pottsdam
Steve's fiancée, sharing the same amnesia and the same missing memories. As the story unravels, her fate becomes directly tied to Steve's choices.
"Mom" (Mrs. Mason)
Steve's mother in Harvest — never given a first name in the game itself, known mainly for baking cookies she never seems to actually offer anyone.
Edna
Runs Edna's Diner in town, alongside her young daughter Karin — one of the only characters who plays it fairly straight.
Colonel Buster Monroe
A legless WWII veteran, deemed too unstable for combat and instead put in command of the Harvest Nuclear Missile Installation.
The Order of the Harvest Moon
The cult-like organization that actually runs Harvest — commonly nicknamed "the Lodge." A Sergeant-at-Arms oversees new members' initiation tasks and, eventually, the truth.

The Ending

By the end, Harvest is revealed to be an elaborate virtual reality simulation, built to test whether an ordinary person can be conditioned into a killer. Steve and Stephanie are the only real people involved — everyone and everything else in town is part of the experiment. Steve is ultimately forced into a binary choice about Stephanie's fate, and which choice he makes decides which of the game's two grim endings plays out.
