  • Harvester was first announced at the Consumer Electronics Show in January 1994 — the finished game didn't ship until September 1996.
  • Writer/director Gilbert P. Austin has said he came up with the entire concept in about 30 minutes.
  • In December 1996, the game's producer publicly petitioned to have Harvester added to a psychologist's list of games linked to violence — widely seen at the time as a publicity stunt rather than a genuine concern.
  • Harvester was banned outright in Germany; reception elsewhere on release was mixed, and it's frequently compared in tone to David Lynch's Twin Peaks and Blue Velvet.
  • The ELIZA effect is still cited today whenever people describe modern chatbots and AI assistants as feeling "understanding" or "empathetic."
  • Weizenbaum's ELIZA had no concept of the actual content of a conversation — it never "knew" anything about Harvester, horror, or you. Dark-ELIZA's offline fallback engine uses the same basic reflection trick, just aimed at a much darker script.
  • Harvester was re-released digitally on GOG and Steam by Nightdive Studios in 2014, nearly two decades after its original release.
